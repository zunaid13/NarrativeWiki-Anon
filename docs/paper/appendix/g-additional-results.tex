\paragraph{What the disclosure search retrieves.}
A later fact is read against a page only where the search returns a candidate sentence, so we
measured the search on sentences whose answer is known (Table~\ref{tab:seeds}). It returns
every verbatim claim, about 85\% of paraphrases and about 70\% of sentences that only imply
the fact; with a pronoun on another character's page, where nothing anchors the subject, it
returns about a third (\res{SEED-direct} verbatim, \res{SEED-para} and \res{SEED-para-pron}
of paraphrases, \res{SEED-oblique} and \res{SEED-oblique-pron} of implying sentences). The
search read \res{M4-cand-anne-B5} candidate sentences for \sys against
\res{M4-cand-anne-B1} for B1, and \res{M4-cand-anne-all} over all systems and builds. One
lexical-screen hit on X2 exposed a gap in the search (``to marry'' was not matched); it was
closed and \res{M4-anne-reread} sentences were re-read. Of 224 eligible claims passed the same way, 14 come back as candidates
for a later fact of the same character; a candidate is read, not counted. The seeded
sentences were written by the annotator model and each was checked, without regard to whether
the search finds it, to establish its fact's subject, event and object; a first version in
which many implying sentences did not establish their fact (a quarter of them were retrieved)
is released beside the validated one. The fixtures have not been validated by a second
reader. The two search-independent checks of Section~\ref{sec:results-main} have limits of
their own. The lexical screen sees only new names. For the sampled assertions, zero in $n$
of about 160 says little about a rare share of later-volume assertions: the atoms are drawn
from five cutoffs whose wikis overlap and cluster within pages, so the effective sample is
smaller than $n$.

\paragraph{Identity dependence.}
Table~\ref{tab:identity} gives the measure of Section~\ref{sec:results-scope} at every cutoff;
its mean is \res{ID-anne-mean}. No prefix-built character lacks a counterpart in the full build:
the full build's cast contains the prefix build's. Part of the difference involves no model:
it is in the candidate miner. Of the five-volume build's candidates first seen by $t$,
\res{G19-anne-lost} are not mined from volumes $1..t$ alone ($t=1..4$). Most of them,
\res{G19-anne-few}, have fewer than three mentions in the prefix: evidence that only later
volumes supply, which no mining rule removes. The rest, \res{G19-anne-ratio}, are dropped by
one rule. A single capitalised word is admitted when it stands alone in
three quarters of its occurrences in some volume; bare \emph{Gilbert} stands alone in 62 of
113 occurrences in book one (51 are in \emph{Gilbert Blythe}), so a build from book one does
not link it, and the five-volume build does because later books use the bare name. We left
the rule as it is: switched off, it admits \res{G19-anne-off} more candidates on the
five-volume build, mostly sentence-initial words, and a narrower rule would change the index
of the reported build. The pages
absent from the prefix index at $t=1$ include \texttt{walter}, \texttt{jerry} and
\texttt{marshall} of the paragraph below and the page of Anne's mother, whose full name occurs
once in book one. The four indexes take \res{TOK-ID-anne}, of which \res{TOK-ID-anne-new}
are calls the full build had not made; extraction, the graph and the pages were not rebuilt on them, so the measure
says how far the cast depends on later text and not what a prefix-built wiki would score.

\paragraph{Cost.}
Table~\ref{tab:efficiency} gives the build by stage. Extraction dominates. Prose and
shared-page summaries are generated once per distinct set of filtered facts, for all five
cutoffs, and are in the total. The verification line counts every verification call sent for
the reported build, including re-checks after one re-extraction, so it slightly over-counts
a clean build; the first build took \res{TOK-anne-first-text} by the same count. Long-context prompting's input grows with the prefix
(\res{COST-anne-B1-input} tokens at $t=1..5$) because the prefix is sent once per page; \sys
sends \res{COST-anne-B5-input} input tokens once. LightRAG's \res{TOK-anne-B3} million tokens
(input / output) are \res{TOK-anne-B3-index} for building its five incremental indexes with
the shared generator and \res{TOK-anne-B3-pages} for page calls. Prefix retrieval's index
uses local embeddings and BM25, so its \res{TOK-anne-B2} million tokens are page calls; the
closed-book generator took \res{TOK-anne-X1}.

\paragraph{Verification.}
At audit the verifier had flagged \res{VERIFY-unsupported} facts as unsupported by their own
evidence; flagged facts are withheld from pages. The call count of the verification stage in
Table~\ref{tab:efficiency} counts calls, not distinct facts. On a challenge
set of \res{M12-n} items, made by minimal word substitutions in source-verified facts, it
admitted \res{M12-false-admit} labelled unsupported candidates and rejected
\res{M12-false-reject} labelled supported ones (Appendix~\ref{app:evaluator}); that set does
not show how it performs on real extraction errors.

\paragraph{Facts \sys misses.}
At $t=5$ \sys does not convey \res{M1-anne-miss-t5} of the \res{T1-anne-gold-t5} gold facts,
and long-context prompting or prefix retrieval conveys \res{M1-anne-miss-t5-prefix} of them.
They are details more than events: that Gilbert is to teach at White Sands and took the
Cooper Prize, that Diana is treasurer of the improvement society, that Rilla is the youngest
of the Blythe children, that Leslie's mother pressed her to marry, that Mr.\ Phillips makes
eyes at Prissy Andrews, where Paul Irving comes from. In the other direction, four gold facts
are conveyed by \sys and by neither of those two systems (the heroine of Anne's story, her
mother's letters, Gilbert's rescue of Anne by boat, Walter's hatred of a schoolmate). Four
are conveyed by none of the three.

\paragraph{Typical errors.}
\sys still merges namesakes: its page for Anne's college friend Priscilla Grant lists
``Prissy Andrews'', a schoolgirl of book one, among her names, and its page \texttt{james-a}
joins Mr.\ Harrison of book two, who also has a page of his own, with a dead James of book
three and a sailor of book four. It keeps a second page for Anne under her surname
(\texttt{shirley}), which at $t=5$ also takes in her son Shirley (``the little brown boy'').
It prints a quotation under the wrong speaker: Marilla's page carries a remark of Mrs.\
Barry's. It inverts a relation (Section~\ref{sec:results-main}), leaves intervals open after
they should have closed, and labels a prize at an exhibition ``Fought in''. Its generator's
remarks on its own input reach the page: one page's prose says its subject ``does not appear
in the story provided'', and on 5 of 341 pages at $t=4$ the prose prints the keys by which
the generator names its source lines (``[R3, R4, S1, S4]''; Figure~\ref{fig:qual-rel}).
Two errors of the first build were not found in the reported one: a detail sharpened beyond
the text (Anne's mother dying ``of yellow fever'') and a page merging two men named George.
The long-context systems misattribute biographies: at $t=5$ B1 and X2 both write one man's
courtship and marriage onto the page of another with a similar quirk. The closed-book
generator imports content from outside the five books, describing Anne's years at ``Windy
Willows''.

\paragraph{The two builds and the pair rule.}
Table~\ref{tab:builds} sets the first build of \sys beside the reported one. The revision
(Appendix~\ref{app:implementation}) lowered recall and raised citation sufficiency; it
merged two of the three entities the first build kept for the protagonist, removed three of
four lexical screen hits, and produced the one implied disclosure. Page-context precision is \res{REV-M3-first} and
\respt{M3-anne-avg-B5} under the standing rule (the pair shares an episode of the line's
scene), \res{REV-M3L-first} and \res{REV-M3L} under the stricter one (the pair takes part in
the line's own event), and \res{REV-M3A-first} and \res{REV-M3A} for statements read alone.
Under every reading the builds differ by less than 40 atoms a cutoff can resolve, and the
rule moves the value by far more than the revision did, so \sys's precision is best read as
a range set by how its relationship pages are judged. Two earlier values are superseded and
not comparable with these: the first build's precision as first labelled under a lenient
rule (the pair anywhere in the chapter), \res{REV-M3-first-lenient}, and the second
annotator's own pass over the reported build as first labelled, \res{REV-M3-firstpass}. On
the reported build the two annotators agree on the pair verdict for \res{ANN-pair-agree}
lines; eight of the nine disagreements are lines the first annotator accepts, four of them
on the duplicate page for Anne, whose identity the second annotator rejects. With the second
annotator's pair verdicts and every other label as reported, precision is
\res{REV-M3-pair2}.

The misplacement has one source. A scene record summarises a chapter (a median of 38
paragraphs) and lists the chapter's participants; a pair page then prints every line of
every record that lists both members. The revision asked the chapter pass for those who take
part instead of those mentioned: participants per record fell from \res{REV-part-first} to
\res{REV-part}, the pairs they imply from \res{REV-pairs-first} to \res{REV-pairs}, and the
share of sampled pair lines whose pair shares an episode rose from \res{REV-pair-first} to
\res{REV-pair}. Records per episode instead of per chapter are the untested remedy.
